smote before or after the cv split? quick

Helper · دستیار

**After the split, and only on the training part.** In cross-validation, that means SMOTE runs inside each fold: fit it on the training folds, then evaluate on the untouched validation fold. If you oversample before splitting, synthetic points are built from neighbours that end up in the validation fold. Those points leak information about validation rows into training, and your CV scores come out too optimistic. In practice, put SMOTE in a pipeline so it's refit per fold. In Python that's `imblearn.pipeline.Pipeline` with SMOTE followed by the model, passed to `cross_val_score` or `GridSearchCV`. A plain `sklearn` Pipeline won't handle resampling correctly. Keep your final holdout or test set unresampled too, so it reflects the real class balance. Does that cover it, or do you want help with a specific setup?

Report PR-AUC as your headline number, and give the 3% base rate next to it. **Why PR-AUC** - With so few positives, ROC-AUC can look good even when the model is mediocre. Its false-positive rate divides by the huge pool of non-churners, so a lot of false alarms barely move it. A model can have a high ROC-AUC and still be wrong most of the time when you act on its top predictions. - PR-AUC tracks what your manager cares about. When the model flags customers as likely to churn, how many of them actually do (precision)? How many of the real churners does it catch (recall)? - A model with no skill scores a PR-AUC equal to the base rate, which is your 3%. So any PR-AUC well above 3% is a clear, easy-to-explain improvement. ROC-AUC's no-skill line sits at the midpoint of its scale, which hides how much room there is. **Caveats** - PR-AUC depends on the churn rate, so don't compare it across segments or periods with different rates. ROC-AUC doesn't have that problem, so it is still fine as a secondary number for ranking quality. - A single AUC is abstract for a manager. If the team will contact a fixed number of customers, add precision and recall at that cutoff. It's the same story in terms they can act on. A line for the slide: "PR-AUC is X against a 3% baseline.
