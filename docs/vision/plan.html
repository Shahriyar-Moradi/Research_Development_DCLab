1. Summary

DCLab has built a strong base over thirteen phases: an engine for most kinds of problem, leakage checks, traceable results, a record of every decision, and agents measured on sealed exams. All of it was measured on written people and invented or public tables. No real data team uses DCLab every week yet, and no company has sent real outcomes back. The vision is about real companies, their decisions and their outcomes, so the plan starts there.

2. Purpose and scope

This document turns the vision into an order of work. It says what is missing, which phase closes each gap, how each phase is judged, and what the owner must decide. It is a plan, not a design: each phase will get its own detailed plan with numbered packages (from 38.x onward), written when the phase starts, in the same way as phases 1 to 13.

Phase numbers continue after phase 13 (Claude first, the lead agent). Every phase follows DCLab's usual method: a test that fails first, the change, the checks, an adversarial review, a development record, and a gate fixed before the measurement.

4. Gap analysis

Each gap is something the vision needs that does not exist or has not been proven. The last column says which phase closes it.

#GapWhy it mattersClosed in
G1No real team uses DCLab weekly.Every later phase needs real data, real decisions and real outcomes. Without users, every measurement stays on invented cases.14
G2No Python SDK. A data scientist cannot write import dclab in a notebook. dclabai is a whole studio, not a small library.The notebook is where data teams work. It is the easiest door into a company.14
G3The lead agent is not proven: it failed its sealed gate on choosing the route and on traps.The first thing a new user meets is the agent. A wrong route loses trust on day one.14
G4No business view. Pages are made for data scientists.The decision-maker needs actions, gains and reasons, not metrics.15
G5Promised gains are never checked against what happened.Without this check, a decision card is an opinion, not evidence.15

G7The outcome loop never ran. Outcomes also arrive late (churn is known weeks later)."Grows with the company" means learning from outcomes. No outcomes, no growth.16
G8Learning bias: acting only on the cases the model picks teaches it nothing about the others.The model would learn only from its own choices and could never correct its blind spots.16

G10No way to share budgets, capacity and rules across models, or to settle models that disagree.Real decisions are limited by shared resources.17

5. The six phases

Sizes are relative (S, M, L), not dates. Dates are set when each phase is planned in detail.

Phase 14 · Real data teams Size L

Put DCLab in the hands of three to five real data teams, through a Python SDK in their own notebooks, and prove the lead agent.

Why now
Every other phase depends on real users (G1). This is also the cheapest phase to learn from.
Builds on
The engine, dclab_core (portable, installs as a wheel), the notebook companion, the tester kit, the open core dclabai, the lead agent of phase 13.
Work
  • The SDK. A small library over dclab_core: check a table or a notebook for leakage and split errors; build a model with the engine; export the code. Three or four calls, no server needed.
  • First teams. Three to five teams on their own problems, running locally with the tester kit, so their data never leaves their machine. A short weekly check-in with each team.
  • Prove the lead agent. Fix the route and trap failures on practice exams only, then one fresh sealed exam. Until it passes, Home keeps its rules.
  • Capture. With each team's consent, record sessions, decisions and corrections, as every phase does.
Out of scope
The decision layer, the cloud, pricing.
Done when
Three teams use DCLab every week for four weeks. In each team, at least one real flaw (leakage, a wrong split, a false score) is caught before export and confirmed by the team. Time from raw data to the first trusted model is measured. The lead agent passes a fresh sealed exam, or the result is recorded and Home stays on rules.
Risks
Finding teams willing to try it; privacy concerns (answered by running locally); the SDK growing too large (keep it to a few calls).
Owner decides
D1 (which teams), D3 (SDK open or private).

Phase 15 · One model, one decision Size M

Turn one model's predictions into a decision card a manager can act on, and check its promise later.

Why now
The pieces exist, so it is cheap, and it is the first thing a decision-maker can see (G4, G5).
Builds on
The operating point from costs and value per case, the cost matrix, capacity-sized lists, uplift, the solution brief's success bar, outcomes linked to decisions.
Work
  • The decision card. Who to act on and how many; the expected gain with its range; the reasons, every number traced; what changes if a cost or a budget changes.
  • The manager's page. One simple page with the card, separate from the data scientist's pages.
  • The promise as a record. Each card stores its expected gain as a decision record, so the real result can be compared with it later.
Out of scope
Several models together; automatic scheduling.
Done when
On three real problems from phase 14's teams, a decision card was used, and its promised gain was later compared with the observed result, whatever that result was.
Risks
Teams cannot state their costs (offer ranges and show how the decision changes across them); outcomes take long (choose problems with short feedback first).

Phase 16 · The live loop Size L

Run a model in use inside one company: score, decide, log, collect outcomes, watch, and retrain.

Why now
Phases 17 and 18 cannot be judged without real outcomes (G6, G7, G8).
Builds on
The code export, the prediction-decision-outcome log, monitoring, the retrain policy with champion and challenger, lineage.
Work
  • Batch scoring first. The exported pipeline runs as a scheduled job (daily or weekly) inside the company. A live API comes later, only if needed.
  • Outcomes in. A simple way to bring outcomes back: a file or a database table, matched to decisions by key.
  • Watch and retrain. Monitoring alerts; retraining only when enough new labelled rows exist; the challenger replaces the champion only if it wins on the newest window.
  • A small exploration share. About 5% of cases, agreed with the company, get a different action, so the model keeps learning about the cases it would not pick.
  • Promise against reality. A regular report: what each decision card promised, and what happened.
Out of scope
Real-time serving; many companies at once.
Done when
One company completes two full cycles (score, decide, outcome, retrain), and the second model beats the first on newer labelled data, with its range. The exploration share is in place and agreed.
Risks
Access to the company's systems; outcome delay; the exploration share has a cost (keep it small, agreed and visible).
Owner decides
D4 (exploration policy), D5 (the first company).

Phase 17 · Many models, one decision Size L

Build the company decision model: several models about the same customer or case, under shared budgets, capacity and rules, turned into one best action.

Why now
It is the heart of the vision (G9, G10), and it can be judged only once phase 16 brings real outcomes.
Builds on
Entity tables (one row per customer at a cutoff), uplift, capacity, the composition of several leaves into one programme, the decision card.
Work
  • One view per case. Every model's prediction about the same customer or order in one place.
  • Constraints. Budgets, capacity, business rules and minimums, written once and shared by all models.
  • Choosing. An optimiser that picks the actions with the highest expected value under the constraints.
  • Disagreements. Clear rules when models disagree, and the reason shown.
  • Invented table first. A replica with planted truth (for example churn, value and uplift for a telecom company), so the right answer is known; then one real company.
Done when
On the replica, it beats each model's own rule by a margin fixed before the run. In one real company, its measured gain beats the previous way of deciding, with its range.
Risks
Uplift needs history where actions varied (phase 16's exploration share provides it); too many constraints make results hard to explain (start with budget and capacity only).

Phase 18 · Grows with each company Size M

Each company gets its own memory and its own small models, trained on its own decisions and outcomes.

Why now
This turns DCLab from a tool into the company's lasting asset (G11). It needs months of decisions and outcomes from phases 16 and 17.
Builds on
The agents' memory (phase 11), the small decision models in shadow and their routing (phase 7), versions with roll-back and the no-forgetting exam, consent.
Work
  • Company memory. Its costs, rules, corrections and past decisions, read by every agent.
  • Company models. Small models trained on the company's own decisions and outcomes, run in shadow first.
  • Promotion. A company model takes over a choice only after it measured better than the general one on the company's new decisions.
  • No forgetting. A frozen exam per company, run before each new version is accepted.
Done when
After several cycles, the company's own model beats the general one on its new decisions, and passes its no-forgetting exam.
Risks
Too little data per company (pool across companies only with consent, never their values); a model that fits the past too closely (judge only on newer data).

Phase 19 · The service Size L

Offer DCLab to many companies, safely, at a price.

Why now
Only after phases 14 to 16 show value in real use (G12).
Builds on
The cloud code in deploy/, accounts and roles, usage limits, the audit trail, consent and retention.
Work
A multi-company cloud; a security review; where data lives; pricing (for example per data team for layer 1, and by decision value for layers 2 and 3); onboarding and support; the line between the open core and the paid service.
Done when
Paying customers, in a number the owner sets before the phase starts.
Risks
Selling to business buyers takes long; security reviews of large companies take long.

6. Order and dependencies

14 Real teams→15 One decision→16 Live loop→17 Many models→18 Company models→19 Service
  • 17 needs 16. A combined decision cannot be judged without real outcomes, and uplift needs the variety that the exploration share brings.
  • 16 needs 14. A live loop needs a real company, and the first company comes from the first teams.
  • 15 can overlap 14. Its pieces exist; it can start as soon as the first team has a model.
  • 18 needs time. It starts once a company has several cycles of decisions and outcomes.
  • 19 can be prepared early (security, pricing ideas), but selling waits for measured value.

Why this order, in one picture. A restaurant: first the kitchen must cook well (14), then the menu must suit the guests (15), then guests must come back (16). Only then does a bigger menu (17), a chef who knows each regular guest (18), or a second restaurant (19) make sense.

7. What continues and what pauses

Continues in every phase

  • Capture: every agent and process records its full data, with the model's thinking summary, for DCLab's own models.
  • The rules of honest measurement: gates fixed before runs, sealed exams read once, promotion only when measured better.
  • The lead agent's fixes, until it passes a fresh sealed exam (part of phase 14).

Pauses until phase 14 has real users (proposed)

  • New internal exams beyond the lead agent's.
  • More small decision models ("students") for new decision points.
  • New storing and archive work beyond keeping what exists running.
  • Phase 9's learning loop, as a separate phase: its useful parts move into phases 16 and 18.

These are good tools, but they do not move DCLab toward the vision now. Real users do.
